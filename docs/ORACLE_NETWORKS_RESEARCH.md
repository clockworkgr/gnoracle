# Economics and Mechanism Design of Decentralized Oracle Networks — Research for a gno.land Oracle DAO

Research date: **2026-09-23**. Method: official documentation, whitepapers, governance proposals and contract source, cross-checked where possible against live chain state (Ethereum/Polygon `eth_call`s, Solana/Pythnet RPC, Tellor Layer and BandChain REST, Blockscout log scans) by seven parallel research passes plus a direct pass on gno.land primitives and consumer fee models. Every numeric parameter carries a source URL. **Verified?** legend: *Yes* = read in an official doc/source or live on-chain; *Derived* = computed from verified inputs; *UNVERIFIED* = only from secondary sources, search snippets, or could not be confirmed (absences are marked as such). Where docs and chain state disagree, the chain value is given and the docs flagged as stale.

Sections: 1 Tellor · 2 UMA · 3 Pyth OIS · 4 Chainlink · 5 API3 · 6 Band & Switchboard · 7 Kleros/Reality.eth · 8 Attack literature & mitigations · 9 Consumer fee models (incl. gno.land read/call mechanics) · 10 Synthesis: recommended parameters and top-5 risks · Appendix A cross-system comparison · Appendix B consolidated UNVERIFIED list.

### Headline findings (read first)

- **Nobody is slashing.** Pyth OIS: zero slashing proposals in two years; Chainlink Staking v0.2: no alert/slash has ever executed (inference from a zero alerter-reward fund); API3 service coverage: designed, audited, never switched on; Tellor Layer: 13 disputes, all "Warning" tier, 11 without quorum; UMA is the only system where the slash actually fires routinely — 0.1% per missed/wrong vote, every 48-h round.
- **Emissions are being cut everywhere and fees have not replaced them.** UMA 0.18 → 0.130 UMA/s (0.095 proposed); API3 staking target 50% → 30%; Pyth OIS reward rate set to 0 in April 2026 after the 100M-PYTH pool depleted; Chainlink staking rewards remain treasury emissions (4.32%/4.5%). Pyth's on-chain update fees earned ~$115K in H1 2026 across 70+ chains and were zeroed; Tellor Layer lifetime tips are 44 TRB. What pays: subscriptions (Pyth Pro $676K gross in Aug 2026; Chainlink Data Streams moved to subscriptions) and sponsor-funded feeds (Chainlink feeds "sustainable" on BNB/Polygon).
- **The dispute-vote whale problem is real and recent.** UMA/Polymarket March and July 2025 episodes (one holder ≈ 25% of votes; top-10 ≈ 30%) led UMA to raise SPAT from 50% to 65%; a WSJ investigation (May 2026) found >50% of votes from the ten largest wallets in most disputed markets.
- **Optimistic windows are short when bots watch and long when humans do.** UMA/Polymarket 2 h with $750 bonds and whitelisted proposer bots; Tellor 360 12 h with 10-TRB disputes; Chainlink's only slashable condition is 3 h of *downtime*, not a wrong value.
- **gno.land specifics that shape the design**: reads via `vm/qeval` are free and cannot be charged; a realm receives coins only through the Banker (`OriginSend` is transaction-wide; a call-scoped `CallSend()` is a draft RFC dated 2026-09-19); gas ≈ $0.0001 per realm call; storage deposit 100 ugnot/byte refundable on delete; no scheduler; `ChainHeight()` and block time are readable; GNOT ≈ $0.062 with mainnet `gnoland-1` live since 2026-09-12.

**(i) Data provision.** [360] Any staker calls `submitValue(queryId, value, nonce, queryData)` for any queryId; **push, single-reporter values, no on-chain median**; incentives are TBR (now 0 on legacy) and Autopay tips — one-time `tip()` for the next report, or recurring `setupDataFeed(reward, interval, window, priceThreshold, rewardIncreasePerSecond)` which is Tellor's heartbeat/deviation knob. Since May 2026 "tipping becomes the primary mechanism for incentivizing reporters on legacy Tellor" (https://tellor.io/blog/tellor-rewards-have-fully-transitioned/). [Layer] Reporters (bonded, ≥1 TRB) report within a per-query block window; `EndBlock` computes a stake-weighted median; a governance "cycle list" is reported continuously and is the only thing TBR pays for; tips are one-time only ("no built-in heartbeats or price thresholds… better handled off-chain"). Data leaves as validator attestations; consumers use push (relayer calls `updateOracleData`) or pull (`verifyOracleData` with attestation + valset sigs).

**(ii) Staking.** [360] one stake = 100 TRB; more stakes = more frequent reporting; 7-day withdrawal lock; slash = one stake per lost dispute. [Layer] the same bonded TRB is delegated to a validator and "selected" to a reporter; no per-report lock (a reporter "can submit ETH/USD once every second"), but past reports remain disputable; 21-day unbonding; 30% caps; ≤5% bonded-stake change per 12 h.

**(iii) Correctness.** [360] every value is presumed valid unless disputed within 12 h; disputed values are removed immediately; consumers must read with a buffer (docs example 20 min; Liquity fix 15 min) and a staleness bound. "Correct" is "at the discretion or interpretation of the Tellor community"; votes can be true/false/invalid. [Layer] two tiers: **consensus data** (≥2/3 reporter power + ≥2/3 validator attestation) usable immediately; **optimistic data** (<2/3) should meet a power floor (>1/3) and age (12 h undisputed); disputed data is never re-attested.

**(iv) Dispute process.** [360] anyone pays 10 TRB within 12 h; fee doubles per open dispute / per round, capped at 100 TRB; 24 h × round voting (≤6 days) + 1-day challenge; 4 groups × 25%; disputer wins the slashed 100 TRB, loser's fees go to the reporter; INVALID refunds everyone but the value stays removed. [Layer] `propose-dispute` with fee = slash (1/5/100% of power); crowdfund within 1 day; reporter jailed + slash escrowed on funding; 2-day vote + 1-day reopen, ≤5 rounds with 10/20/40/80% non-refundable round fees; 3 groups × 33.3%; >50% at ≥51% participation finalizes. Empirically 11/13 mainnet disputes ended without quorum (team never voted).

**(v) Slashing.** [360] reporter loses one stake to the disputer; failed disputes send the fee to the reporter; no voter payout or burn. [Layer] 1/5/100% by category, escrowed and paid to winners as *staked* TRB (so disputes can't be used to exit); jail applies to every selector in the report snapshot; validators 1%/5%; voters share 2.5% of R1 fee and half of later-round fees.

**(vi) Rewards & fees.** Ethereum still mints 146.94 TRB/day to the oracle (bridge) and 146.94 TRB/day to the team; Layer mints 146.94 TRB/day (75% reporters / 25% validators). Tips 98/2 (Layer) or 2% to stakers (360 Autopay). ≈3.7%/yr issuance, no cap, changeable only by fork. Lifetime tips on Layer: 44.4 TRB — i.e. **the fee side is negligible; the system runs on inflation**.

**(vii) Attacks & mitigations.** *Bad value inside the window* ([360]: cost = one 100-TRB stake ≈ $2k; mitigation = consumer buffers + dispute monitors). *Dispute griefing/censorship* (disputing removes good values; fee doubles per open dispute on a queryId; "disputed reporter gets the malicious dispute fee"; the **Liquity 2022 issue** — Liquity read Tellor's latest value with no delay and disputes could push reads back to a stale value; fixed with a 15-min delay, https://www.liquity.org/blog/tellor-issue-and-fix). *BonqDAO Feb 2023*: attacker staked 10 TRB on Polygon, posted WALBT = $5,000,000 then $0.0000001; Bonq consumed "the latest price data without allowing time for validation" (https://blockapex.io/bonqdao-hack-analysis/). *51% capture* ([360] needs 3 of 4 groups incl. team; reporter votes are non-transferable history). *Stake-USD manipulation* (derived from Tellor's own 12-h-old price, bounded, itself disputable). [Layer] *validator-set capture*: cost ≈ 2 × bonded × price ≈ $2.3M today; mitigations 5%/12 h stake-change limit, 30% caps, evidence-based slashing, bridge guardian after 21 days, 10,000-TRB bridge-pause proposal. *Under-reported optimistic queries*: "if only 100 TRB worth of honest stake knows how to dispute your query, an attacker could… submit bad values if they have >100 TRB". *Tip/vote farming*: 2% tip burn; TBR only on cycle list. *2023-12-31 TRB pump/dump* (+~150% then −75% in hours; $68M liquidations; Synthetix ~$2M loss) was a market event, not a mechanism failure (https://cointelegraph.com/news/tellor-altcoin-pumps-dumps-causes-speculation-over-cause).

**(viii) Token role.** Stake for reporters/validators, tipping currency, dispute fee/slash asset, dispute-vote weight (360: holders directly; Layer: only via reporter selection or tips), Cosmos `x/gov` token on Layer. The team keeps one dispute-vote seat ("acts as a tie breaker… can and should be forked out"), bridge-pause approval, and the 146.94 TRB/day dev share. No treasury module; 360 contracts are immutable.

**(i) Price-request flow.** OOv2: requester calls `requestPrice(identifier, timestamp, ancillaryData, currency, reward)`; a **proposer** posts `bond + finalFee` with a value; during liveness anyone may **dispute** by posting the same amount, which forwards the request to the DVM and immediately pays `finalFee + bond/2` to the Store; undisputed → settles at liveness end (https://docs.uma.xyz/protocol-overview/how-does-umas-oracle-work). OOv3: `assertTruth(claim, asserter, callbackRecipient, escalationManager, liveness, currency, bond, identifier, domainId)`; callbacks `assertionResolvedCallback` / `assertionDisputedCallback`; an **escalation manager** can `blockAssertion`, `arbitrateViaEscalationManager`, `discardOracle` (disputed = false without DVM), or `validateDisputers` (https://docs.uma.xyz/developers/optimistic-oracle-v3/escalation-managers). Proposers are overwhelmingly bots; since Aug 2025 Polymarket uses ManagedOOv2 where only whitelisted addresses propose but "Disputes remain 100% permissionless". UMA has no push feeds — everything is pull-per-request, and the DVM is only invoked on dispute.

**(ii) Bonds.** Proposer = disputer bond by construction. "Burned" in UMA code means *sent to the Store* (DAO treasury), not destroyed. OOv3's minimum bond is 2× final fee so the 50% burn always covers the final fee. Worked example (Sept 2026 Snapshot): 45 × (250+250) + 41 × (250+125) = 37,875 USDC.e to the Store from 86 disputed requests.

**(iii) DVM 2.0 voting.** Stake in VotingV2; emissions accrue continuously pro-rata; commit 24 h / reveal 24 h; delegation 1:1. Resolution needs **GAT** (≥5M UMA participating) **and SPAT** (≥65% of all staked UMA agreeing); otherwise the request "rolls" (`RequestRolled`) to the next round; after >4 rolls a non-governance request is deleted. UMIP-186 rationale: attacker "needs 66% of staked UMA instead of 51%"; 14 rolled votes in the prior year; only 2% of 2025 requests would have been affected. No abstain on price requests — not revealing = slashed.

**(iv) Slashing.** `slash = ceil(effectiveStake × 0.001)` per request for DidNotVote and WrongVote; correct voters receive `effectiveStake × totalSlashed / totalCorrectVotes`. Compounding is linear: ~6 votes per round on average, "sometimes more than 20" (UMIP-188 thread) → a fully absent staker loses ~0.6–2% per 48 h round. Parameters unchanged since 2023 (same library address). OpenZeppelin's audit (https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/uma-dvm-2-0-audit) found 2 criticals (over-rewarding in `updateTrackersRange`; duplicate slashing across rolled votes), a "parasitic staker" who accumulated rewards "without ever being slashed" (fixed), and an unbounded `unstakeCoolDown`.

**(v) Dispute process.** Anyone with the bond disputes (unless an OOv3 escalation manager restricts disputers). Timeline: liveness (2 h) → dispute → next 48-h round → 48–96 h; Polymarket adds a 24–48 h "debate period". Polymarket's adapter resets on the *first* dispute (fresh proposal round) and escalates to the DVM only on the *second*; a P4 "too early" DVM answer also resets; admins can `flag` + `resolveManually` after a 1-h `SAFETY_PERIOD` (https://github.com/Polymarket/uma-ctf-adapter). Settled OOv2 requests cannot be re-proposed.

**(vi) Rewards & fees.** Requester posts the reward (may be 0); proposers earn reward + half the loser's bond; the Store earns final fees + half of losing bonds *only when disputes occur*. Voter compensation is almost entirely **emission-based inflation** (~4.1M UMA/yr) plus redistributed slashes; Risk Labs additionally pays gas rebates. Emission cuts (2025–26) are UMA's main fee-model lever.

**(vii) Attacks & mitigations.** *Whitepaper CoC > PfC*: "the smallest possible bribe that can alter the verification system's behavior… Cost of Corruption… must exceed… Profit from Corruption"; the fee-funded buyback/PfC registry it describes is **not implemented** in DVM 2.0 (code reading — UNVERIFIED as a documented statement) (https://github.com/UMAprotocol/whitepaper). *Cost vs value secured today*: 65% × 19.45M ≈ 12.65M UMA ≈ $5.4M at spot vs single markets of $242M (Zelenskyy suit). *Whale capture incidents*: "Ukraine mineral deal" (Mar 2025, $7.08M volume) — one holder with ~5M UMA across three wallets cast ~25% of votes (https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/03/27/polymarket-uma-communities-lock-horns-after-usd7m-ukraine-bet-resolves); "Zelenskyy suit" (Jul 2025, $242M) — "top ten UMA voters… hold roughly 6.5 million UMA, ~30% of the average UMA participation per vote"; critics: "UMA's voting incentives encourage people to vote with the perceived majority… not based on factual correctness" (https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/07/09/this-isnt-decentralized-says-polymarket-power-user-as-zelenskyys-suit-controversy-unfolds); WSJ (May 2026): in most disputed markets >50% of votes from the ten largest wallets; Strategy-bitcoin market lawsuit filed 2026-07-03 (https://ethnews.com/polymarket-lawsuit-over-strategy-bitcoin-market-tests-oracle-model/). *Bond-trap griefing (Aug 2026)*: 7,605 zero-reward lookalike requests with 31.7-year liveness injected through Polymarket's adapter trapped proposer-bot bonds; one bot lost 55,250 USDC.e and self-disputed 86 to recover half; DAO refund passed 2026-09-09 (https://snapshot.org/#/uma.eth). *Early proposals as the dominant dispute cause*: "The far majority of disputes are not necessary to correct market resolution, but rather are due to early proposals or incorrect disputes"; disputes "increasing roughly 3x in the last year" (UMIP-188 thread). *Mitigations in force*: commit-reveal ("prevents lazy voters from copying other voters"), 0.1% slash + redistribution, 7-day cooldown, GAT + SPAT 65% with rolling, whitelisted proposers, escalation managers, EmergencyProposer/GovernorV2 recovery; proposed but not adopted: higher final fee; research (with Polymarket/EigenLayer, Feb 2025): multi-token resolution, dynamic bonds/liveness, bonded AI proposers.

**(viii) Token role.** Only staked UMA votes; every parameter (`setEmissionRate`, `setUnstakeCoolDown`, `setGatAndSpat`, `setSlashingLibrary`, `setMaxRolls`, Store final fees, OOv3 `setAdminProperties`) is set by UMIP → GovernorV2; 4,000 UMA bond for treasury proposals; UMA is *not* a whitelisted bond currency on mainnet.

**(i) Data provision.** First-party publishers (exchanges, market makers) push (p, c) every 400 ms slot to Pythnet, a PoA appchain where each publisher runs a validator; publishers "publish their updates for free". Consumers **pull**: Hermes serves Wormhole-attested Merkle proofs; anyone posts them with `updatePriceFeeds`; reads like `getPriceNoOlderThan` revert with `StalePrice` if too old (https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/why-update-prices). Pyth Pro/Lazer replaces this with a permissioned off-chain router (median + IQR confidence, 1 ms–1 s channels) gated by subscription token (https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/pro/how-lazer-works).

**(ii) Staking.** Every publisher gets a pool; anyone delegates PYTH to it. The soft cap C_p rewards publishers in proportion to the number of symbols they serve, with the Z term (max(n_s, 10)) preventing thin-publisher symbols from inflating caps beyond M/Z = 180,000 PYTH per symbol. Self-stake is filled first; delegators get the remainder minus a 20% fee. Weekly epochs; stake activates next epoch; 1-epoch cooldown.

**(iii) Correctness.** Not decided by voting on individual values: the Pythnet aggregation (median of 3 votes per publisher) bounds any <25% faulty coalition. Integrity breaches are adjudicated *ex post* by the Pythian Council using the rulebook's numeric test (≥250 bps for ≥60 s), against reference venues.

**(iv) Dispute process.** No formal disputer role or bond: community/affected protocols raise a forum thread, the Council collects evidence, publishes a recap, and executes `create_slash_event` via the 7-of-9 multisig; anyone can then call `slash` per delegation record. Deadline: same or following epoch.

**(v) Slashing.** ≤5% of pool (self + delegated) per event, pro rata; delegators are explicitly co-liable; slashed funds to DAO custody (DAO may compensate victims). Never exercised.

**(vi) Rewards/fees.** Rewards were a non-inflationary but *finite* subsidy (100M PYTH; ~1.93M/week) that depleted in ~19 months; the DAO chose not to backfill from revenue and instead routes Pyth Pro subscription revenue to a PYTH Reserve buyback (one-third of treasury monthly; ~12M PYTH bought). On-chain pull fees earned only ~$115K in H1 2026 across 70+ chains and were then zeroed to avoid "double-dipping" with off-chain subscriptions.

**(vii) Attacks & mitigations (documented).** Publisher collusion → median-of-votes p25–p75 guarantee, permissioned publisher set, min publishers per feed, monthly ranking, slashing. Thin-market manipulation → publisher diversity, confidence intervals, consumer guidance to size trades by conf/price. Stale pull prices → `getPriceNoOlderThan`, hold times, commit-reveal against latency arbitrage (https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/best-practices). Confidence misuse → 2021-09-20 BTC/USD print to $5,402 (two publishers printed near-zero; absolute-dollar confidence weighting over-weighted them); fix: relative weighting, integration tests, "use both price and confidence" (https://www.pyth.network/blog/pyth-root-cause-analysis). Delegator concentration → soft caps and uniform fee; key compromise → per-feed publisher permissioning (no dedicated policy doc: UNVERIFIED).

**(viii) Token role.** PYTH = governance (1:1 coin vote via staking program), OIS collateral, and Reserve buyback target. Councils hold delegated operational powers incl. OIS pool-capacity parameters, delegation fee, and "determination of slashing amounts".

**(iii) Correctness.** Enforced by the BFT median (faulty oracles can't move the median outside honest observations when f < n/3) plus on-chain verification of f+1 signatures. **Staking v0.2 does not adjudicate value correctness at all** — the only slashable condition is *liveness* (no report for >3 h on ETH/USD), checked purely on-chain from `updatedAt`.

**(v) Slashing.** Operators only, principal, 700 LINK each; community never slashed in v0.2; v0.1 slashed only accrued rewards. Slash capacity is rate-limited (`_getRemainingSlashCapacity`).

**(vi) Rewards & fees.** Staking rewards = treasury emissions (4.32%/4.5%), explicitly not protocol fees. Feeds are funded by a **sponsor / shared-cost model**: "any number of users can consume and fund the same feed… once user fees surpass the network's operation costs… the feed becomes economically sustainable" — already true on BNB Chain and Polygon (https://chain.link/blog/sustainably-growing-chainlink). Plus SCALE (chains pay operating costs), BUILD (3–7% of project token supply), GMX-style revenue share (1.2% of GMX V2 fees), and since 2025 Payment Abstraction routing fees into a LINK Reserve. SVR captures liquidation OEV (>$1.1M recaptured by Aug 2025; https://x.com/chainlink/status/1960401960663748934).

**(vii) Attacks & incidents (documented).** Node collusion → f < n/3. Human/config error → XAG/USD returned gold price 20–21 Feb 2020 (https://blog.synthetix.io/update-on-xag-pricing-incident/); Moonwell wrsETH/ETH mis-feed 4 Nov 2025, $3.7M bad debt (https://forum.moonwell.fi/t/wrseth-oracle-malfunction-11-4-25/2017). Congestion staleness → Black Thursday 12 Mar 2020 ETH/USD stalled ~6 h (https://cryptobriefing.com/chainlink-experiences-6-hour-delay-eth-price-feed/); mitigations OCR single tx, transmitter rotation, consumer staleness checks. Circuit-breaker failure → LUNA/USD floored at ≈$0.10 May 2022, Venus ~$11.2M, Blizz ~$8.3M (https://therecord.media/collapse-of-luna-cryptocurrency-leads-to-11-million-exploit-on-venus-protocol); min/max now disabled on most feeds. L2 sequencer downtime → uptime feeds + 3,600 s grace. OEV/front-running → Data Streams commit-reveal, SVR private order flow with public fallback (https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/svr-feeds). Sponsor default → shared-cost + SCALE; no published SLA (UNVERIFIED).

**(viii) Token role.** LINK = payment unit and staking collateral; no governance vote; parameters set by Chainlink Labs / node-operator multisigs.

**(i) Question flow.** Asker posts a question with bounty, arbitrator, timeout and (v3+) min bond. Anyone answers with a bond ≥ 2× the previous; each answer resets the timeout; when the timeout passes with no new answer the question finalizes and `claimWinnings` replays history newest→oldest. Optional commit-reveal defends against front-running. `reopenQuestion` handles "answered too soon".

**(ii) Bonds & payouts.** The final correct answerer receives the bounty plus all earlier incorrect bonds minus the 2.5% claim fee; anyone can pay the arbitration fee to freeze the question and send it to Kleros; if the arbitrator overturns the current answer, the requester becomes payee and receives the last (wrong) bond. Kleros' own design note: "At some point, it eventually becomes profitable to pay the arbitration fee to win that bond" (https://blog.kleros.io/the-kleros-realit-io-oracle/).

**(iii) Court mechanics.** Jurors stake PNK per court; draw probability ∝ stake; each drawn vote locks `minStake × alpha`; first round 3 (proxies request 31/15/5); appeals 2n+1 with exponentially rising fees; court jump at threshold; periods evidence → (commit) → vote → appeal → execution. v2 General Court uses hidden votes (commit-reveal); v1 General Court is public.

**(iv) Redistribution.** Incoherent jurors lose `α · minStake · weight`; the pool is split equally among coherent jurors together with the ETH juror fees. Non-voting = incoherent.

**(v) Attacks & mitigations (documented).** *51%/whale*: "would need to buy enough PNK… requiring hundreds of millions of dollars"; ultimate defense "forking the system to remove the attackers' holdings" (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kleros/kleros-docs/master/kleros-faq.md). *Bribery / p+ε*: "the attacker would have to keep bribing larger and larger juries at a steeply rising cost"; p+ε "requires a high budget but has zero cost if successful"; defenses: appeal escalation (copying round-1 majority "does not guarantee coherence with the ultimate result"), mixed-strategy responses, hidden votes; empirically failed on Doges on Trial. *Appeal griefing*: exponentially rising cost; loser pays 1.7× vs winner 1.3× and has half the window. *Reality.eth griefing*: bond escalation is bounded by the arbitration option; timeout griefing mitigated by min bond, 2.5% claim fee and `max_previous`; front-running by commit-reveal. *Ambiguous questions*: KIP-72 created a dedicated Oracle Court with refuse-to-arbitrate rules.

**(vi) Fee model & token.** Asker pays bounty (+ optional arbitrator question fee); answerers post bonds; arbitration requester pays `feeForJuror × nJurors` (≈0.17–0.5 ETH for 31 General Court jurors depending on parameter epoch); appellants pay appeal cost × multipliers; coherent jurors earn ETH fees + redistributed PNK. PNK = sortition weight, Sybil resistance, governance (KIPs set minStake/alpha/feeForJuror), fork defense. v2 (Arbitrum, beta): Dispute Kits, appeals/crowdfunding handled by the court, cross-chain arbitration, `pnkAtStakePerJuror = minStake × alpha / 1e4`.
## 8. Schelling-point oracle attack literature and standard mitigations

### 8.1 Foundational posts

**SchellingCoin** (Buterin, 28 Mar 2014, https://blog.ethereum.org/2014/03/28/schellingcoin-a-minimal-trust-universal-data-feed). Mechanism: commit in an even block ("submit a hash of the ETH/USD price together with their Ethereum address"), reveal next block, sort, and "Every user who submitted a correctly submitted value between the 25th and 75th percentile gains a reward" (median is the output). Weaknesses noted in the post itself: >50% control "can basically unilaterally set the median"; a ~1% coalition can pre-announce a false value and bribe ("0.00001 schells to whoever votes for that value") — the seed of P+ε; "micro-cheating" inside the 25/75 band; fixes: unambiguous definitions ("the last ask price of ETH/USD on exchange XYZ at time HH:MM:00"), coarse-grained values, participants with opposing incentives.

**The P + epsilon attack** (Buterin, 28 Jan 2015, credit Andrew Miller, https://blog.ethereum.org/2015/01/28/p-epsilon-attack). Honest payoff matrix: vote with the majority → P, else 0. Attacker credibly commits to pay P+ε to anyone who votes 1 *if the majority votes 0*; voting 1 becomes weakly dominant, everyone votes 1, the condition never triggers, so "the attacker will not need to pay anything at all" — takeover "at zero cost". Mitigations discussed: multi-round adjudication ("use round N + 1 to determine who should be rewarded during round N"); **security deposits + escalation** (credited to Sztorc: "require participants to put down a large deposit, and build in a mechanism by which the more contention there is, the more funds are at stake"); mixed-strategy counter-coordination (unstable); and the promise of "subjective" resolution (forks). Conclusion: "Schelling schemes too may well simply end up working in practice, even if they are not perfectly sound in theory."

Related: **Notes on Blockchain Governance** (2017, https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/17/voting.html) — "the probability that any given voter will have an impact on the result is tiny, and so the personal incentive that each voter has to vote correctly is almost insignificant"; obfuscated bribes via exchanges; Lisk cartels ("LiskElite" 55 members) that "explicitly bribe coin holders". **On Collusion** (2019, https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2019/04/03/collusion.html) — "much harder, and more likely to be outright impossible, to make mechanisms that maintain desirable properties in a model where participants can collude"; Sybil "controlling thousands of fake identities… is even easier than… bribing people".

### 8.3 Attack vectors: definition → real example → standard mitigations (and who uses them)

**P+ε / bribery / collusion.** Credible conditional bribe flips the equilibrium at zero cost. Examples: Steem/Tron Mar 2020 (exchange-custodied stake used to rig votes; community forked to Hive, https://decrypt.co/38050/steem-steemit-tron-justin-sun-cryptocurrency-war); Lisk cartels; the live Kleros bribe experiment (final round 24–98 against the bribe). No confirmed pure P+ε takeover of a production oracle found. Mitigations: deposits so the bribe must exceed the deposit (Kleros `minStake × alpha`; UMA 0.1%; Augur bonds; Chainlink dn²/2); appeals to larger juries (Kleros 2n+1; Augur doubling; Tellor fee doubling; UMA OO→DVM); fork as last resort (Augur 2.5%/60 d; Tellor team "can and should be forked out"; Hive); commit-reveal (UMA 24/24 h; Kleros hiddenVotes; Truthcoin); token-price cost of corruption (UMA: a 65% corrupt vote would make "the UMA token… depreciate significantly").

**Lazy voting / freeloading / herding.** Copying the visible majority or another feeder's answer. Example of the correlated-source failure it produces: Synthetix sKRW 25 Jun 2019 — one of two APIs "1000x higher" (https://blog.synthetix.io/response-to-oracle-incident/). Mitigations: commit-reveal (Chainlink v1, UMA, Kleros v2); reputation/outlier weighting (Truthcoin SVD, 20/80 smoothing); slashing incoherent voters; appeals that make copying round-1 unsafe; time-limited reveals.

**Whale capture / plutocracy.** Examples: Polymarket "Ukraine mineral deal" (Mar 2025) — ~5M UMA across three wallets ≈ 25% of votes (https://www.theblock.co/post/348171/polymarket-says-governance-attack-by-uma-whale-to-hijack-a-bets-resolution-is-unprecedented); "Zelenskyy suit" (Jul 2025, >$200M) — top-10 voters ≈ 30% of participation; blog claims of "10 wallets control over 60%" UNVERIFIED. Mitigations: stake caps (Chainlink 15,000 LINK per community address; operators 1,000–75,000); multi-constituency weighting (Tellor 4×25% → 3×33.3%); random juries (Kleros); participation/agreement thresholds (UMA GAT 5M + SPAT 65%, raised after the March incident); quadratic weighting (proposed by Buterin; no production oracle found — UNVERIFIED); fork/social override.

**"Nothing at stake" for voters.** Voters bear no cost for absence or carelessness (Buterin 2017). UMA DVM 1.0's participation problems motivated DVM 2.0's slashing "so a non-participant nets 0% APY" (https://github.com/UMAprotocol/uma-docs/blob/master/protocol-overview/dvm-2.0-faq.md). Mitigations: slash absent voters (UMA 0.1%/vote; Kleros non-voters = incoherent); lock stake during votes (Kleros locked tokens; UMA 7-day cooldown; Truthcoin); roll votes that miss quorum.

**Oracle front-running / OEV.** Trading around a pending update. Example: Aave liquidation MEV — Chainlink SVR "recaptured over $1.1M in non-toxic liquidation MEV… over $32M in liquidations" by Aug 2025 (https://x.com/chainlink/status/1960401960663748934). Mitigations: commit-reveal (SchellingCoin, Chainlink Data Streams); OEV auctions (API3 80% to dApp; Chainlink SVR 65/35 with Aave, fallback after 5 blocks); private order flow; MakerDAO OSM 1-hour delay (`hop = 3600`, https://github.com/makerdao/osm/blob/master/src/osm.sol); deviation/heartbeat batching.

**Data-source manipulation (flash loans / thin markets).** bZx Feb 2020 ($350k + $635k); Harvest 26 Oct 2020 (~$24M via $50M USDC flash loan, https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2020/10/26/harvest-finance-24m-attack-triggers-570m-bank-run-in-latest-defi-exploit); Compound 26 Nov 2020 (DAI to ~$1.30 on Coinbase Pro, sole reporter; "85.2m DAI were repaid by liquidators", anchor "within 20% of the Uniswap TWAP", https://www.comp.xyz/t/dai-liquidation-event/642); Cream 27 Oct 2021 (~$130M, yUSD share-price donation, https://immunefi.com/blog/bug-fix-reviews/hack-analysis-cream-finance-oct-2021/); Inverse 2 Apr 2022 ($15.6M, thin SushiSwap INV/WETH feeding a Keep3r TWAP, https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/inverse-finance-02-april-2022); Mango 11 Oct 2022 (~$116M, thinly traded MNGO, https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-13); Solend 2 Nov 2022 ($1.26M, single Saber pool via Switchboard, https://www.coindesk.com/business/2022/11/02/defi-protocol-solend-struck-by-126m-oracle-exploit); BonqDAO 1 Feb 2023 (10-TRB Tellor stake, WALBT $5,000,000 then $0.0000001, https://blockapex.io/bonqdao-hack-analysis/). Mitigations: TWAP (with the multi-block caveat); multi-source medianization; anchors/deviation bounds (Compound ±20%); confidence intervals (Pyth: use the lower bound for collateral); minimum liquidity thresholds; dispute delay before consumption (Tellor); stablecoin price caps (Chainlink USDC maxSubmissionValue $1.05); borrow/supply caps.

**Liveness failures / stale data.** MakerDAO Black Thursday 12–13 Mar 2020 (Medianizer "$166 when the real price was around $130"; zero-bid auctions; ~$8.3M ETH for 0 DAI, https://research.glassnode.com/what-really-happened-to-makerdao/); Chainlink ETH/USD ~6 h stall the same day; Venus/Blizz LUNA 13 May 2022 (feed floored at $0.10 minAnswer; ~$11.2M + $8.3M, https://rekt.news/venus-blizz-rekt); Chainlink node gas-griefing 30 Aug 2020 (~700 ETH, https://www.theblock.co/post/76986/chainlink-nodes-attack-eth). "Chainlink Solana feed pause on Mango" — UNVERIFIED and likely incorrect (Mango was a manipulation). Mitigations: heartbeat + deviation; consumer `updatedAt` checks with pause/fallback; L2 sequencer uptime feeds (3,600 s grace); removing min/max circuit breakers; redundancy of sources; larger auction lots/longer auctions (Maker).

**Dispute griefing / bond-escalation griefing.** Frivolous disputes to delay or impose cost. Examples: Polymarket bad-faith proposals/disputes "purely to delay resolution by 3-5 days"; UMA Aug 2026 bond-trap (7,605 zero-reward lookalike requests); Liquity/Tellor 2022 (disputes pushing reads back to stale values). Mitigations: bond doubling per round (Augur, Tellor, Reality.eth); loser pays (UMA 50/50 split; Reality 2.5% claim fee); capped rounds (Tellor Layer 5; UMA delete after 4 rolls; Augur fork threshold); dispute fees compensating jurors ("avoid an attacker from spamming the system", Kleros); quarantining rather than deleting disputed values; consumer read buffers.

**Sybil / stake dilution, key compromise, governance capture.** Sybil: staking selection is "open to sybil attacks by design" (SoK) — mitigated by stake-weighting and minimum stakes (BonqDAO shows a 10-TRB minimum is too low). Key/admin compromise: Chainlink feed proxies behind a 4-of-9 Safe (https://ackee.xyz/blog/chainlink-data-feeds/); API3 4-of-4 / 4-of-8; mitigations: timelocks, multi-oracle fallbacks, transparent upgrade paths. Governance capture of parameters: Beanstalk 17 Apr 2022 — flash-loaned voting power passed a malicious proposal, $182M (https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2022/04/17/attacker-drains-182m-from-beanstalk-stablecoin-protocol); mitigations: snapshot-based voting power, timelocks, quorums, bounded parameter deltas, forkability.

### 9.2 View reads vs state-changing reads — the mechanics on gno.land

Facts (verified against gno.land docs, 2026-09-23):
- Read-only queries are free and off-chain: "Using `Call` to call an exported function will use up gas, even if the function does not modify on-chain state… you can use the `query` functionality for a read-only call which does not use gas" (`vm/qeval`) (https://docs.gno.land/users/interact-with-gnokey/). A realm therefore **cannot charge for a qeval read** — there is no transaction, no sender and no coins.
- Inside a transaction, a realm can receive coins only through the Banker: `BankerTypeOriginSend` gives "full access to coins sent with the transaction that called the banker"; `OriginSend()` "Returns the Coins that were sent along with the calling transaction"; `BankerTypeRealmSend` lets a realm move "coins that the realm itself owns" (https://docs.gno.land/resources/gno-stdlibs/). `OriginSend` is **transaction-wide**, not per call, so an oracle realm called by a consumer realm cannot tell whether the `-send` was meant for it; a call-scoped receipt, `CallSend()`, is a draft RFC (PR #6213, 2026-09-19; unmerged) (https://github.com/gnolang/gno/pull/6213).
- Gas: minimum price floor "1 ugnot/1000 gas", dynamic; a realm call is budgeted around 2,000,000 gas → ≈2,000 ugnot = 0.002 GNOT ≈ **$0.00012** at $0.062/GNOT (https://docs.gno.land/resources/gas-fees/ ; https://coinmarketcap.com/currencies/gno-land/). Storage deposit "100ugnot" per byte (~1 GNOT per 10 KB), locked on write and refunded on delete, "tracked per realm (not per user)" (https://docs.gno.land/resources/storage-deposit/). Realms can read `ChainHeight()` and the block timestamp (https://docs.gno.land/resources/gno-stdlibs/).
- Only EOAs initiate transactions; there is no scheduled execution in the stdlib (https://docs.gno.land/resources/realms/) — consistent with the "no cron" constraint.

Implications:

1. **Pure-view reads (qeval, or a consumer's view helper)** — treat as a public good, exactly as Chainlink/API3 do. The only ways to monetise them are indirect: (a) *sponsor activation* — a feed only stays "active" (providers rewarded, liveness slashing armed) while a subscription balance is funded; (b) *access control* — the read function refuses callers not in an allowlist (Chronicle's "kiss"), which works even under qeval because the function body still executes, but it forfeits composability and anyone can proxy the value once obtained; (c) *delayed public tier* — the freshest value is only returned to paying callers, and a delayed copy is public (API3's 60-s-delayed public signed data is the precedent). UNVERIFIED: whether raw realm storage is queryable through ABCI without going through an exported function; assume any stored value is effectively public.

2. **State-changing reads inside a consumer's transaction (`MsgCall` → consumer realm → oracle realm)** — the oracle *can* charge, in three ways: (a) **prepaid credit ledger**: the consumer realm (or its sponsor) calls `Deposit()` with `-send`; the oracle credits `PreviousRealm().Address()`; every `GetPrice()` debits a per-read fee and reverts when the balance is empty (subscription-with-metering; no dependency on `CallSend`); (b) **realm-pays-on-call**: the consumer realm uses a `RealmSend` banker to transfer the fee to the oracle realm immediately before calling `GetPrice()`, and the oracle checks its balance delta — fragile and reentrancy-sensitive; prefer (a) until `CallSend()` lands; (c) **update-and-read (Pyth pattern)**: the caller submits a fresh signed provider value together with its read and pays for the update; on gno.land gas is so cheap that this saves nothing, but it lets a consumer force freshness without cron.

3. **Fee sizing reality check.** With gas at ~$0.0001 per call, an oracle fee of 0.01–0.05 GNOT ($0.0006–0.003) per state-changing read is already 5–25× the gas cost and still trivial for a lending protocol; Pyth's target of $0.01–0.015/update earned ~$19K/month across 70+ chains. **Plan for subscriptions, not micro-fees**: a feed-activation subscription (e.g. 1,000 GNOT / 30 days ≈ $62) is the number that keeps providers paid; per-read fees are a metering signal and an anti-spam measure, not the budget.

4. **Storage deposits change the cost structure**: every stored round costs the writer ~0.01–0.02 GNOT in locked deposit (100–200 bytes). Use ring buffers per feed and prune finalised rounds/votes so deposits are refunded; otherwise provider costs grow linearly with history.
## 10. Synthesis — recommended starting parameters for a gno.land Oracle DAO

### 10.1 Constraints and what they imply

| Constraint | gno.land fact (cited in §9.2) | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Single chain, no cron | only EOAs start txs; no scheduler | every deadline is a timestamp/height comparison evaluated lazily inside the next call (`Poke`, `Read`, `Dispute`, `Settle`); every liveness penalty needs an *alerter* who is paid |

| Providers stake GNOT; DAO token holders adjudicate | brief | two collateral pools: provider GNOT (slashed for data faults) and DAO-token stake (slashed for absence/incoherence, UMA/Kleros style) |

### 10.2 Architecture in one paragraph

Per-feed **push with deviation + heartbeat** (Chainlink/API3 pattern, §4/§5) from N ≥ 3 staked providers; on-chain **stake-weighted median** over each provider's latest fresh submission (Tellor Layer / Pyth pattern, §1B/§3) with per-provider weight capped at 30%; every individual submission is **disputable for a window**; the aggregate is exposed in two tiers — **consensus** (≥ 2/3 of feed stake weight within tolerance → usable immediately, Tellor Layer's 2/3 rule) and **optimistic** (thin or divergent → usable only after the dispute window, Tellor 360 rule); disputes are decided by **DAO-token stakers under commit-reveal with slashing for absence and incoherence** (UMA DVM 2.0), with one **appeal round at higher quorum** (Kleros/Tellor escalation) and an **INVALID fail-safe**; consumers pay via **feed-activation subscriptions plus metered state-changing reads** (§9).

Derived sanity checks at launch scale (5 feeds × 5 providers, 25,000 + 4 × 10,000 = 65,000 GNOT ≈ $4,000 per provider; ≈ $20K provider collateral; DAO stake say 5M tokens):
- Cost to corrupt one feed's consensus tier ≈ two colluding providers at 30% weight each is *not* enough for a weighted median (> 50% needed), so ≥ 3 of 5 providers ≈ $12K of stake at risk for a single bad round; consumers should therefore keep `MaxValueAtRisk` per feed ≤ ~$6K until stake grows — publish this number.
- A voter with 20% of DAO stake who deliberately votes wrong on one dispute loses 0.25% of stake; to *win* they need 60% of revealed weight, i.e. at least 3 such actors under the cap, and would face an appeal at 55% with the same cap — the whale must win twice.

### 10.4 Top 5 design risks and how the parameters address them

1. **Whale/plutocratic capture of dispute votes** (UMA Mar & Jul 2025: 25–30% of votes from 1–10 wallets; WSJ May 2026; §2, §8.3). Mitigations: commit-reveal (#12); 20% per-address cap (#17); 60%/55% supermajorities with INVALID fallback (#13); two-round appeal (#14); slashing symmetric and modest so herding is not rewarded (#16); consumer-side rule that a disputed value stays quarantined so a captured vote can at worst *freeze*, not *inject*.

2. **Nobody disputes / liveness of the watcher set on a new chain with no cron** (Tellor Layer: 11 of 13 disputes ended without quorum; Tellor: "if only 100 TRB worth of honest stake knows how to dispute your query, an attacker could… submit bad values"; §1B). Mitigations: 50% of slash to the disputer and 50% of liveness slash to the alerter (#8, #9); low absolute bond minimum with crowdfunding (#6); treasury share earmarked for watcher bounties and voter gas rebates (#20, Risk Labs pays ~$45k/month in UMA rebates); absence slashing so voters show up (#15); consensus/optimistic two-tier so consumers of thin feeds wait the full window (#5).

3. **Stake too small relative to value secured** (BonqDAO 10-TRB stake; UMA 65% of stake ≈ $5.4M vs $242M markets; §2, §8.3). Mitigations: USD-targeted minimum stake with GNOT floor (#1); per-feed stake add-ons (#1); published `MaxValueAtRisk` per feed (§10.3 sanity check); 100% Major slash + ejection (#7); outlier quarantine so one round cannot print an absurd value into the consensus tier (#4); parameter timelock so the floor cannot be quietly lowered (#23).

4. **Dispute griefing / censorship-by-dispute** (Liquity/Tellor 2022; UMA Aug 2026 bond-trap; early-proposal disputes "increasing roughly 3x"; §1, §2). Mitigations: disputed values are quarantined, not deleted — the last good consensus aggregate remains readable (#4/#5); bond doubles per open dispute per feed and per round, capped at the provider's stake (#6); failed dispute pays the provider 50% (#10); INVALID consumes 5% so nuisance disputes are never free (#11); appeal bond 2× with 25% consumed on loss (#14).

5. **Economic unsustainability / reward cliff** (Pyth OIS y → 0 after 100M PYTH; API3 target cut 50 → 40 → 30%; UMA emissions 0.18 → 0.130 → 0.095 proposed; Pyth on-chain fees ≈ $115K/half-year then zeroed; Tellor tips 44 TRB lifetime; §1–§5, §9). Mitigations: fee-first split with providers at 70% (#20); subscriptions as the budget and per-read fees as metering (#21); capped, decaying bootstrap tied to real subscriptions (#22); treasury 15% to smooth; no protocol inflation.

Secondary risks worth tracking: provider collusion/freeloading on the same upstream API (require ≥ 3 distinct declared sources per provider and rotate spot checks; API3's first-party model and Chainlink's three-layer aggregation are the references); P+ε bribery of DAO voters (deposits + commit-reveal + appeal are the standard defences, and GovDAO/social fork is the last resort — cite §8.1); front-running of updates on gno.land (deviation thresholds and the 10-min delayed public tier limit it; an OEV auction is a later addition); admin-key/upgrade risk of the oracle realm itself (timelocked upgrades, parameter bounds, published multisig threshold).
